01 / listening lab

Listen. Notice. Repeat.

Read the mini dialogues and use your browser's voice to hear the English sentences.

EVERYDAY ENGLISH

Meeting someone new

A short conversation you might hear on your first day somewhere new.

Alex: Hi! Is this your first day here?Friendly question
Sam: Yes, it is. I'm a little nervous.Sharing a feeling
Alex: Don't worry. Everyone is really nice.Offering reassurance
LISTENING TIPS

Make every listen count

  • First, listen for the general idea. Don't stop at every unknown word.
  • Listen again and notice words that connect ideas.
  • Repeat one sentence. Copy the rhythm, not just each sound.
  • Try to explain the conversation in your own words.

Your mini mission: Can you remember one question and one reassuring phrase from the dialogue?

02 / reading orbit

A little story, a new perspective.

Read for the main idea first. Then look for details that support it.

SHORT READING · A2–B1

The little observatory

Every Friday evening, Maya visits the small observatory near her town. She used to think that space was too far away to feel real. Then one night, she looked through a telescope and saw the rings of Saturn. They looked like a tiny drawing in the dark.

Since that evening, Maya has kept a notebook. She writes down questions, sketches what she sees and searches for answers. She does not understand everything yet, but she enjoys learning something new each week. For Maya, curiosity is a journey — not a race.

Check your understanding

Why does Maya keep a notebook?

04 / grammar station

Make the pieces fit.

Grammar gives your ideas a clear structure. Start with this quick present simple challenge.

QUICK GUIDE

Present simple

Use the present simple for habits, routines and facts.

I / You / We / They

I study English every day.

He / She / It

She studies English every day.

Remember: with he, she or it, the verb usually takes -s or -es.

YOUR TURN

Choose the correct sentence:

“My brother _____ football on Saturdays.”

05 / speaking studio

Find your voice.

You don't need perfect English to communicate. Start with one idea and build from there.

SPEAKING PROMPT

Describe a place where you feel relaxed.

Try speaking for 30–60 seconds. You can use these starters:

“I feel relaxed when…” · “One thing I like about this place is…” · “I usually go there with…”

06 / writing workshop

Put your thoughts into words.

Write a short paragraph. This space stays in your browser and is not sent anywhere.

WRITING CHALLENGE

Write about something you want to learn.

Try to include: what it is, why it interests you, and one step you could take to get started.
